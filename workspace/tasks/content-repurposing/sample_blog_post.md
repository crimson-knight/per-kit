# Why we moved our maintenance crews to battery equipment

By Sam Rivera, Harbor & Pine Landscaping. Posted September 18, 2026.

Two years ago, every mower, trimmer, and blower on our trucks ran on gas. This spring we
finished moving our three maintenance crews to battery equipment. Here is what happened,
including the parts that did not go to plan.

The first reason was noise. Several of our commercial clients are medical offices and a
Montessori school, and they had asked us to stop running blowers before 9 a.m. Battery
blowers are quiet enough that two of those clients lifted the restriction, and our crews
can start their routes at 7:30 again.

The second reason was fuel and repairs. In 2024 we spent about $11,800 on gas and $4,300
on small-engine repairs across the three crews. For the first full season on battery
equipment, our charging cost came to about $1,100 on our shop's electric bill, and repairs
dropped to about $900, most of it for one trimmer head.

It was not free. The batteries, chargers, and tools cost us about $26,000, and we bought
eight more batteries than we first planned because the crews kept running out on long
mowing days. We also added a charging rack to each truck that runs off a second alternator,
which took a week of downtime per truck to install.

The surprise was the crews. We expected pushback, and there was some for the first month.
By June, the crews told us they preferred the battery tools because there was less
vibration and no fumes at the end of the day. One crew lead said his hands stopped going
numb after long trimming days.

What we would do differently: buy the extra batteries up front, install the truck racks in
winter when work is slow, and start with the blowers, because that is where clients notice
the difference first.

If you run a crew and are thinking about switching, the honest summary is this: it costs
more up front, it pays for itself through fuel and repairs within about two seasons at our
size, and the quieter work opened doors with clients who care about noise.
